What does PRAGMA deploy on Day 1?

Total Lawn Care has been cutting commercial turf in Northeast Florida since 1985. Four decades of the same discipline: mowing, trimming, irrigation, installation, and maintenance for hospitals, municipalities, HOAs, and commercial accounts across the Jacksonville market. The Hawkinson family runs it — two generations in the same business, 45 operators out of Green Cove Springs.

Bob Hawkinson is PRAGMA's first external client. The relationship was built the way the right ones are built: between operators who understand what it costs to run a real business over a long time. PRAGMA's thesis has always been that the operating knowledge is the moat — not the technology. TLC is the proof of concept. What Bob has that can't be purchased is forty years of commercial landscape relationships in one of the fastest-growing metros in the Southeast. The technology is infrastructure. The knowledge is irreplaceable. IBM's 2026 research found that only 9% of business leaders are currently running a Frontier Firm — despite the operating advantages being well documented. The gap isn't access to AI. It's access to the domain knowledge required to deploy it against a real business.

On Day 1, PRAGMA deployed nine agents on a dedicated M4 iMac at TLC's office. Those agents have been running for 19 days.

What does a 40-year-old small business look like from the outside?

Before building anything new, PRAGMA audits what exists. You don't rip out a foundation before you understand what it's holding. Today — Day 19 — that audit is complete.

Five findings. In plain language:

  • TLC ranks #1 for their money keyword. "Commercial lawn care Jacksonville FL" — first position. #1. That's a decade or more of domain authority accumulating in one place. You cannot buy it on Day 1 of a new site. It is the most valuable digital asset the business owns, and it would be trivial to damage it with a careless migration.
  • Their website loads in 16.5 seconds on a phone. Google's threshold for good performance is 2.5 seconds. TLC's site loads in 16.5. That is not a content problem — the content is fine. It's a bloat problem: accumulated plugin residue from years of WordPress updates that nobody cleaned out. This is the single biggest lever in the build. Fix the speed, and rankings improve without changing a word.
  • They rank #10 in their own hometown. TLC is headquartered in Green Cove Springs. They rank tenth for local searches in the town their trucks leave from every morning. The fix is mechanical: one dedicated service-area page written for that geography. It will rank within 30 days of going live.
  • The Agri-Community doesn't exist online. TLC recently opened a nursery operation — the Agri-Community. The URL returns a 404. The project has no pages, no signals, no traffic. This is a new revenue line with zero digital presence.
  • 15 Yelp reviews. TLC has served Mayo Clinic. They've maintained municipal contracts across Clay County for decades. They have the kind of client roster that takes forty years to build. They have 15 Yelp reviews. This is a reputation gap that runs directly counter to what the business has earned in the real world.

None of these findings are catastrophic. One of them — the speed problem — is a 48-hour fix with material consequences. The rest are content and presence gaps that a structured build closes one by one.

40 years of domain authority isn't something you buy. It's also not something you migrate carelessly.

Why fix the existing site before building a new one?

An AI deployment built on top of a broken digital foundation is just faster failure. Industry data consistently shows the same pattern: most AI projects fail not because the AI doesn't work, but because they launch before the underlying system is ready. Fix first. Then build. That's the sequencing change the audit forced.

The new website was always part of the plan. But a new website takes time to get right — Bob approves every page before it goes live, and that's the correct approach. You don't ship a new front door without the owner walking through it. What the audit surfaced is a set of fixes that can go live before the new site is finished: the speed repair, the Green Cove Springs service page, the Agri-Community buildout, and a review velocity protocol. Google's Core Web Vitals data has been a direct ranking signal since 2021 — the 16.5-second load time is an active penalty, and fixing it takes 48 hours. Some of these will move rankings before the new site ever publishes.

Backend fixes go live as we complete them. The new website doesn't launch until Bob approves it. The rankings don't wait.

Scout, Lens, Atlas, and Pulse are already running on Bob's iMac. They're watching competitors, monitoring the web, staging briefs. The moment Bob grants access to Google Search Console and Google Business Profile, those agents wire into real data — actual ranking positions, click-through rates, search impressions, review velocity. Until then, they're working from public signals. The infrastructure is live. The data access follows.
