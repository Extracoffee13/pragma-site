The two layers.

Every PRAGMA install ships two things: a reasoning bench for judgment and a Flock for persistence. Neither substitutes for the other. Together they behave like an operations team that doesn't sleep.

When most people think about AI agents for their business, they picture one thing: a smart assistant they open when they need it, ask a question, get an answer. That's not wrong — it's just incomplete. The session-based model handles one category of work well. It handles another category almost not at all.

The category it handles well: judgment. Writing a competitive analysis. Drafting a strategy memo. Building a financial model. Producing a brand asset. These tasks benefit from a capable model, context about your business, and a human in the loop who can steer. The Operators handle this. Engage them, do the work, end the session.

The category the session model handles poorly: persistence. Watching your Google reviews around the clock and responding within the hour. Monitoring three competitor websites daily for pricing changes. Checking whether your top-ten ranking pages drifted down this week. Syncing your operational dashboard every morning before you open your laptop. These tasks don't need judgment. They need a system that runs continuously, fires on schedule, and produces output whether anyone is at the keyboard or not.

That's the gap PRAGMA's architecture is built to close. Two layers — both on your machine, both owned by you.

Layer one: the Operators.

The eight PRAGMA Operators — Keystone, Vector, Atlas, Lens, Scout, Pulse, Prism, Ledger — are the reasoning bench. Each owns one function of the business with a documented charter: what it does, what it doesn't, what triggers it, what it produces. Custom Operators are built per engagement for domain-specific operations.

The Operators work when you work with them. That's the correct model for judgment-heavy tasks — you want a human principal in the loop on strategy calls, competitive positioning, financial decisions, creative output. The Operators are how you bring frontier-model capability to those decisions without hiring the team that used to make them.

What the Operators are not: a surveillance layer. They won't notice a one-star review at 11pm while you're asleep. They won't catch the moment a competitor drops their pricing page. They won't flag a GSC indexation failure before it compounds. That's not a criticism of the model — it's just not what judgment-based agents are for.

Layer two: The Flock.

The Flock is the operations floor. Twenty to thirty lightweight utility agents running as background processes on your machine on defined schedules. No session. No human trigger. No dependency on whether you're at your desk.

  • Review Monitor — New review lands on Google or another platform → response drafted and queued within the hour. Same-hour response rate, every day, without staff involvement.
  • Competitor Watcher — Three to five named competitors checked daily. Pricing pages, new service pages, personnel changes, product additions. The alert is in your inbox before you open your laptop.
  • GSC Drift Alert — Google Search Console monitored daily. Ranking drops, indexation failures, and crawl errors surface before they compound into traffic loss.
  • Dashboard Sync — Live operational dashboards refresh automatically. Portfolio metrics, client pipeline, compliance status — always current without manual data entry.
  • AI Citation Probe — Checks whether Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity cite your business when answering buyer-intent queries. Score posted to your dashboard daily.
  • Custom Flock agents — Five to ten additional utility agents built for your specific operations during the install phase. Defined at discovery; deployed at Week 5.

The Flock is what makes the system feel alive between Operator sessions. It's also what makes the "24/7" claim real rather than aspirational — the Operators work business hours because you do. The Flock works all hours because your business has no off switch.

Your competitor's pricing page changes at 2am. Your Flock catches it. The alert is in your inbox before you open your laptop.

Why you need both.

Operators only
Smart when you use it.
Capable reasoning when engaged. Silent when not. Reviews go unanswered overnight. Competitor moves go unnoticed until you remember to check. The work is only as good as the operator's memory for what to ask.
Operators + Flock
Smart and vigilant.
The bench handles judgment. The Flock handles persistence. Between Operator sessions, the business is still being watched — reviews monitored, signals caught, dashboards current, alerts staged for morning. The system runs whether you're at the keyboard or not.

Most AI platforms sell you one layer and call it complete. Session-based assistants (Cyndra, Lindy, most enterprise AI tools) handle the Operators category reasonably well. What they don't ship is the Flock — the persistent background layer that runs independently, on schedule, without requiring the client to log in.

That's not an oversight on their part. Running persistent background processes on a client's own machine requires a different architecture than a hosted SaaS platform. It's harder to build, harder to deploy, and harder to charge for in a per-seat model. PRAGMA builds it because the alternative — a system that only works when you use it — isn't an operating system. It's a productivity tool.

The ownership structure.

Both layers are installed on the client's machine. Both are owned by the client from day one.

structural guarantee

Your agents run on your machine with your API keys. Your data never touches PRAGMA infrastructure. If PRAGMA ceased operations the day after handoff, nothing would stop working.

The Operators are configured on the client's own AI platform. The Flock runs as background processes under the client's operating system. At handoff, PRAGMA delivers the agent map, the charters, the configurations, and the Flock source in full.

This matters because most AI platforms take the opposite approach: agents hosted on vendor infrastructure, data routed through vendor APIs, subscription required to keep anything running. When the subscription lapses, the agents stop. The business built a dependency, not an asset.

PRAGMA's handoff is the asset. A running system you own, both layers, that you could operate independently the day we leave — and that the retainer makes better as the network learns.

How the retainer makes both layers better.

The monthly retainer after Week 8 isn't "we'll answer your questions." It's the mechanism by which both layers improve continuously.

When a new Flock agent works well on one client — a better competitor-watch method, a more accurate citation probe, a faster dashboard sync — it ships to the next client's install. When an Operator produces a better output pattern on a dental practice, that pattern becomes part of the standard dental-practice kit. The system learns at the individual level through the Self-Improving Loop. It learns at the network level through the retainer.

Client one gets a system built from what PRAGMA knows today. Client ten gets a system built from what PRAGMA learned across every engagement before theirs. The retainer is what makes that possible.

Frequently asked.

Do I need to manage the Flock once it's installed?
No. The Flock runs on defined schedules with defined outputs. You consume its output — the alerts, the dashboards, the daily briefs — without managing the agents directly. PRAGMA handles updates during the retainer period. If an agent breaks or a source changes, the retainer covers the fix.
What if I already have a SaaS tool doing one of these things?
That's a discovery conversation. Some tools are worth keeping. Most Flock agents can be built to replace a SaaS subscription outright — same output, no monthly fee, runs on your machine. PRAGMA maps your current SaaS stack in Phase 01 and recommends what to consolidate. The consolidation math is significant.
Can I add Flock agents after the install?
Yes. The retainer includes ongoing Flock expansion as new monitoring needs surface. Clients typically add three to five new Flock agents in the first ninety days after stabilization as their operating model shifts around the system.
Why don't other platforms offer the Flock layer?
Running persistent background processes on a client's own machine requires a different architecture than hosted SaaS. It's harder to build and requires the client to own their own infrastructure. Most platforms prefer the hosted model because it creates recurring dependency. PRAGMA's design choice is the opposite — client-hosted, client-owned, client-operated. The Flock is one expression of that choice.
